## §2 · The three corrections §12 demanded of the shipped lattice

**1 · Everything is integer, including `dev`.** The shipped lattice accumulates load in fixed point and then relaxes a **float** `dev` — so the stored object, the one the whole design is arranged around keeping sparse, was the single thing that could not be reproduced across a compiler flag. QC-1 and QC-2 measured 22 percent FMA divergence between contraction settings.

Here `dev`, `baseline` and `capacity` are `int64` in minor units × 2^16, and the relaxation coefficients are **dyadic rationals over one power-of-two denominator**. That constraint is what buys exactness: the step becomes integer arithmetic with a single truncating division, and truncation is the same on every machine. A non-dyadic denominator is refused at `init`, not at the first sweep **[M: `total.non_dyadic_coefficients_refused`]**.

**This is stronger than the blueprint claims, and it is delta 1.** §5 places the sweep in the *bounded* tier — agreement to a stated envelope, no verb flips. Integer-only arithmetic places it in *exact and portable* instead. Measured here as bit-identical across runs with the same iteration count **[M: `sweep.bit_identical_across_runs`]**; R2's host-versus-device harness is the test that decides whether the stronger claim survives a different compiler and a card, and until it runs the blueprint's weaker tier is the one to quote.

**2 · The projection is differential, and bit-identical *by construction*.** The field remembers exactly what each cell contributed and where, so a reprojection subtracts that stored contribution and adds the new one. Integer addition is associative and commutative, so any order of updates reaches the same lattice — checked by placing the same set in reverse and comparing digests **[M: `diff.order_independent`]**. The shipped code recomputed the whole lattice on every append, which QC-2 measured as the dominant cost.

**3 · The sweep terminates.** A **relative** tolerance, `tol · max(1, ‖dev‖∞)`, under a ratified iteration cap that is recorded on the result rather than hidden. QC-2 measured the shipped absolute tolerance parked at one ULP on a large field. A field given one iteration reports `capped` **[M]**.

## §3 · The deadline wheel

§3.4 asks that "a tick re-slots only the cells whose deadline crosses a slot boundary, found by a deadline wheel". A cell at slot *k* crosses into *k−1* the moment `now` passes `due − k·slot_ns`, so it is filed under that instant and a tick touches only the cells that actually move — measured at a fraction of the placed set per tick **[M: `wheel.but_not_all_of_them`]**. Cells with no deadline are never in the wheel at all.

**The `+1` is load-bearing.** The first draft filed a cell at `due − k·slot_ns` exactly. A deadline sitting precisely on a slot boundary is then re-filed at the instant that just fired, the tick loop picks it up again, and the process hangs — which it did, for the full 600-second timeout, on the first run. The crossing instant is the first one *with the smaller slot*, one nanosecond later, and the wheel now refuses to file anything at or before `now` **[M]**.

And the wheel key is **stored, not recomputed**: the key a cell was filed under depends on the `now` at filing time, so recomputing it at removal time — after a tick has moved `now` — looks in the wrong bucket and leaks the entry. Every differential must undo exactly what it did, which means remembering it rather than deriving it again under changed conditions.

## §4 · Two defects the lie arms found

**The conservation check could not see the failure it exists for.** The first version excused cells the field had not placed — `if (!placed_.count(c.id)) { ++skipped; continue; }` — so a commitment the projection quietly lost was skipped on both sides and conservation held. The lie arm plants exactly that loss, and it passed. Every contributing cell now counts on the table side whether or not the field placed it **[M: `cons.lie.a_lost_commitment_shows`]**. A conservation law that forgives the missing is not a conservation law.

**The warrant flag has to be counted, not set.** A lattice cell is a boundary condition while at least one warrant sits in it. Setting the flag on placement and never clearing it means the last warrant leaving freezes the cell forever — the sweep skips it, and a cold rebuild disagrees with the incremental one. The counts are per lattice cell now, and they are in the field's digest, because a warrant changes what the next sweep computes **[M: `warrant.cleared_when_the_last_one_leaves`]**.

## §5 · Totality

Every accumulation is overflow-checked before it happens: an amount that cannot be represented is refused with a typed reason and **leaves no residue** — the placement fails whole, the lattice is untouched, and the cell is not recorded as placed **[M: `total.a_refused_placement_leaves_nothing`]**. A fold that wraps is a fold that lies.

## §6 · Deltas against the blueprint

1. **The sweep may be exact and portable, not merely bounded** (§2 above). Claimed only as far as measured: bit-identical across runs on one build. R2 decides the rest.
2. **Relaxation coefficients must be dyadic rationals** over a common power-of-two denominator. §3.4 gives `omega`, `decay`, `k_cls`, `k_slot` and a tolerance without saying what a ratified value may be; this is the constraint that makes the integer sweep possible, and the shipped defaults (1, 1, ¼, ¼) already satisfy it.
3. **The baseline's time constant is a shift**, `1/2^shift`, so the EMA step is exact and the tick conserves pressure — value moves from `dev` into `baseline` and their sum is unchanged **[M: `wheel.the_tick_conserves_pressure`]**.
4. **The field's digest excludes `F_DIRTY` and includes the warrant counts.** A digest that moved when a bookkeeping bit flipped would not be a fold identity; one that ignored a boundary condition would not be complete.
