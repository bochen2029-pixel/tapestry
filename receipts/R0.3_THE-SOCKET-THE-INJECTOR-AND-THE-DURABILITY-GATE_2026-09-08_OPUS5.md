## §2 · Who holds the record

An acknowledgement is only evidence if the thing recording it outlives the thing that sent it. So the client here is the **parent** process and the transactor is a **child**: the parent remembers every `(request_id → pos, h)` it was told, kills the child — often from inside a write — and then checks the tape it left behind against what it was promised. A harness that keeps its ledger inside the process being killed is measuring nothing.

```
== 33 passed, 0 failed ==      exit=0
```
**[M]** 1,000 kills, 2,000 process boots, 26,701 acknowledged writes, about six minutes on one desktop.

Read in order:

- **No acknowledged entry is ever lost, and none ever changed** — 26,701 of them, checked twice per iteration: once on the raw post-crash tape, again after a clean reopen.
- **Every recovered tape verifies**, zero breaks across 2,000 reopens.
- **A retried write applies once.** Not one retry produced a second entry. This now survives a crash, which it did not at R0.2 — see §5.
- **Every unacknowledged survivor is deduplicated: 445 of 445.** These are writes the client sent and never got an answer for, which the operating system landed anyway. **Two thirds of unanswered writes survived** (445 of 666) — this is not an edge case, it is the normal outcome of a process kill. The client cannot tell a survivor from a loss; it retries, and the store hands back the original position instead of writing the money twice.
- **221 kills landed inside a `WriteFile`**, leaving a genuine torn row, and recovery truncated every one of them. R0.1's 200 real kills hit that window **zero** times.

### The correction to the gate's wording

§9 asks that 1,000 kills "lose no acknowledged entry and **keep no unacknowledged one**". The second half is not achievable under a *process* kill and never was: the OS holds written-but-unflushed bytes and lands them after the process is gone, so a batch that was written and not yet flushed survives as entries nobody was told about. That is not a defect; it is what at-least-once means one layer down, and it is why §4.9 says exactly-once is never claimed.

The achievable property, equally strong and measured above:

> **No acknowledged entry is ever lost, and every unacknowledged survivor is deduplicated by the reply cache when the client retries — so the world never sees the write twice.**

This was written into the R0.1 receipt's §8 before the harness existed, rather than discovered at the gate. **[R: R0.1 §8]**

## §4 · The injector reaches what 200 real kills could not

R0.1's kill harness landed **zero of 200** kills inside `WriteFile`: with a flush per batch the writer is parked in `FlushFileBuffers` almost all of its wall time, and even a 200 KB write returns faster than the kill's scheduling window. The torn-row recovery path — the one that matters most — was reachable only by hand-editing a file.

`TAPESTRY_FAULT=torn_write:<n>:<bytes>` writes part of the nth row and calls `TerminateProcess` on itself: no unwinding, no flush, no atexit. That is what a crash mid-write actually looks like, and it happens on demand — **221 times in the run above, and recovery truncated all 221** **[M]**.

The lesson generalises past this store: **a timing-based crash test measures the schedule, not the code.** Two hundred kills said the torn-row path was unreachable; it took a switch that makes it certain to learn that the path works.

## §5 · What building the gate found

**1 · The idempotency key was not on the tape, so "a retried write applies once" held only until the first crash.** R0.2's reply cache was in memory, keyed `(client_id, request_id)`. §4.2 says the client reply cache "is part of the replicated state machine" — that is, a fold, not a side table. It could not be one, because the tx body did not carry the key. It does now, and the fold rebuilds the cache on open, so a transactor that has just recovered can still tell a retry from a new write. Without this the gate is measuring an in-memory map that the kill destroys **[SPEC, and delta 1]**.

**2 · A redelivered suffix is guarded twice, and one guard is not enough.** Falsifier 2's second lie is "deliver one twice". The ingest law already refuses a *repeated* fact — a revision equal to `src_pos_last` is a counted duplicate no-op — so a redelivery test built on identical facts passes with no other guard at all, and passes for the wrong reason. A suffix that **regresses** a cell's revision is the case only a position guard catches: the revisions are not equal, the fact applies, the cell walks backwards, and the next row in the suffix re-adds its increase to the exposure cap. The fold now refuses anything at or below its applied position **[SPEC, and delta 2]**.

The oracle's lie arm shows both halves: with the guard rewound, the cap moves — and **the cell table's digest does not**, because replaying a suffix ends on the same last write per cell. That is falsifier 5's scoping exactly ("state-bearing folds return to their digest; accumulating folds gain exactly the rows the transaction produced"), and it is why a redelivery test that watches only the cell table sees nothing. The damage lives in the accumulator, which is where the money is **[M]**.

**3 · The verifier could not tell "died mid-row" from "broken".** Torn trailing bytes made `verify_dir` report a break, so every crashed tape looked like corruption and the gate failed on tapes that were behaving exactly as designed. Torn bytes in the **last** segment are recoverable — `open()` truncates them, counts them and writes a `warn` row — while the same bytes in an earlier segment are a real break, since a segment that has been rolled past can no longer be the one being written. The report now separates `torn_tail_bytes` from `breaks` **[SPEC, and delta 3]**.

## §6 · The wire

Four calls: `write`, `tick`, `health`, `head`. The other eight of §4.8's twelve are **absent, not stubbed** — a call that answers with a placeholder is worse than one that is missing, because a client will believe it.

A frame is a 4-byte big-endian length and that many bytes. Three properties, each an oracle **[M]**:

- **A declared length above the 8 MiB cap is `too_large`**: the header is read, the body never is, and no allocation happens on a stranger's say-so. The connection is dropped and the fault is counted on `health`.
- **A malformed body is a counter-class fault and the connection survives it.** Protocol noise does not become a claim on the tape, and it does not cost the caller its session either. Same for an unknown call.
- **A retry over the wire returns the same position.** Idempotency is a property of the store, not of the client's memory.

The request body for a write is byte-for-byte the body that will be hashed into the entry, parsed by `read_tx_body` — the same strict fixed-order reader the tape uses. One encoding, one reader, two places: there is no second grammar to drift.

## §7 · Deltas against the blueprint

1. **`client_id` and `request_id` are fields of the `tx` body.** §3.1's `tx` body carries `facts[]`, `effects[]`, `judgment_ref` and `basis_pos`, and no idempotency key — but §4.2 requires the reply cache to be part of the replicated state machine, which is only possible if the key is on the log. The two sections disagree; this resolves it in §4.2's favour.
2. **A fold refuses anything at or below its applied position**, and reports what it refused. Implied by §4.9's at-least-once delivery and falsifier 2, stated nowhere.
3. **`torn_tail_bytes` is not a break.** §4.2 describes the recovery; the verifier's contract never said what a torn tail should be called, and calling it a break makes every crash look like corruption.
4. **Four calls, not twelve** — the other eight wait for the folds and the judge that would give them something true to say.

Carried forward and still open: R0.1's `seg`/`warn` kinds, the ghost rule, the `$b64` tag, the v0 float policy; R0.2's `Cell` arithmetic, `amount_minor`, `not_authored_by_seam`, and the cap's contribution rule.

## §8 · What R0 does not do, now that it is finished

The transactor is one thread, not §4.1's three. The reply cache is unbounded — §4.8's open question 7 (client timeout and retry policy) has to answer before it can be trimmed safely, because a cache that forgets a request before the client stops retrying re-admits the write. `subscribe`, `ack`, `query`, `schema`, `explain_refusal`, `verify_fold` and `replay` are absent. Nothing here has been measured under concurrent clients: one connection is served at a time, which is honest for a single writer but is not a throughput story.

R1 is next: the state, field, baseline, caps and grades folds on the host, differential; the 128-byte cell across the full 64-bit change set; the per-class pins against the real class map; and conformance on the Olist wire.
